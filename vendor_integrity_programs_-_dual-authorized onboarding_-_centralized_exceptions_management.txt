Vendor Integrity Programs


Dual-Authorized Onboarding

The vendor master file is the "gatekeeper" to the company's bank accounts. If an employee can freely add a fake vendor, they can easily steal money.
• First-Level Validation: When a request to add a new vendor is submitted, a dedicated compliance officer (independent of procurement) must verify the business's legitimacy. This includes checking active state registry filings, verifying IRS tax status, and checking global sanctions lists.
• The Secondary Sign-Off: Once validated, the vendor file remains "pending" or "inactive" in the ERP system. It cannot be used to issue a PO or process a payment until a secondary finance manager reviews the compliance documentation and signs off to activate the profile. This eliminates single-point vulnerability.

Centralized Exceptions Management

"Emergency" or "Sole Source" procurement paths are the most common vehicles for high-value fraud, as they bypass standard competitive bidding rules completely.
• Defining True Emergencies: Tighten internal policies to strictly define what constitutes an emergency (e.g., an active facility flood or a critical system outage). Wanting to hit a year-end budget deadline is not an emergency.
• Executive-Level Elevators: Any request to bypass competitive bidding must automatically escalate out of the standard procurement loop. It should require documented justification and written sign-off from the CFO or Chief Compliance Officer.
• Retrospective Auditing: Maintain a centralized register of all sole-source exceptions. This log must be audited quarterly by the board to identify any patterns of specific departments or buyers overusing emergency paths to favor particular vendors.
